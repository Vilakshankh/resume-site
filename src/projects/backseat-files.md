# Backseat Files

An interview series with designers and creatives in Toronto.

Backseat sits in the passenger seat of someone else's creative practice. Each conversation curates a guest's work and walks back through how it was made: what went in, what they were trying to do, and what came out the other side. The aim is to learn how the creative process actually works, from the people doing it.

## Why it exists

- **Education** — understanding creative process by studying it closely, one artist at a time, rather than guessing at it from finished work.
- **Thought leadership** — turning each conversation into content that adds something new to how people talk about making things.
- **Network** — building real relationships with Toronto's creative community through the series itself.

## How guests are found

Guests come from creative events around the city. Backseat starts in rooms where people are already gathered around the work, and the conversations grow out of those meetings.

## The hypothesis

> You can categorize creative work by the artist's expressive intent, and then use that to predict the form of the final result — or more specifically, the emotion it conveys.

Put simply: if you know what an artist is trying to express before they start, you should be able to anticipate what the finished piece will look like, sound like, or feel like. Intent comes first, form follows, and the emotion a piece carries is the clearest trace of that intent.

![Diagram: curated inputs flow into tools and systems, which produce form and emotion. Expressive intent shapes the inputs and predicts the output.](/projects/backseat-files/input-process-output.svg)

Every interview is a test of this idea. Each guest's work becomes a data point: what did they set out to express, what shape did the result take, and what does it make people feel?

## Research questions

The hypothesis breaks down into six questions that shape every conversation.

- **How artists curate their inputs.** What they choose to take in — references, influences, environments, people — and just as importantly, what they choose to leave out.
- **What their creative goals and outputs are.** What they are trying to achieve with a piece, and what they actually end up making.
- **Whether inputs and outputs can be grouped by intent, or by something else.** The core of the hypothesis. If intent is the right way to sort creative work, patterns should appear across guests. If it isn't, the interviews should reveal what the real organizing principle is.
- **The tools, techniques, and systems they build for their process.** The methods, rituals, and setups artists build to make their work repeatable, or to deliberately break their own habits.
- **How they define and relate to risk, and how it feels when it works or fails.** What risk means to each person, how much of it they take on, and what success and failure feel like from the inside.
- **How they imitate other artists.** Where influence turns into imitation, how artists learn by copying, and how they eventually move past it into something of their own.
