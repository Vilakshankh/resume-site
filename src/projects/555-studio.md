# 555 Studio

A creative experimentation studio from Montréal, Québec — designing spaces, formats, and experiences that bring people into deeper conversation, guided by curiosity and attention.

**Role:** Co-host, Design · **Founded:** 2024 · **Based in:** Montréal, QC

> Brilliant people in isolation leads to wasted potential.

## Overview

555 started from a single hypothesis: Montréal is full of brilliant people — scientists, entrepreneurs, artists, people who change the way we think — but most of them never end up in the same room. 555 exists to close that gap, bringing people with different backgrounds, products, ideas, and ideologies into one place and giving them a reason to talk about what they actually care about.

The studio treats creation broadly. Creating doesn't have to mean software or a physical product — it can be something intangible: a spark between two strangers who walk in alone and leave as co-founders or collaborators, or a feeling shared between three or more people in a group. At 555, **the community itself is the thing being designed.**

## The hypothesis

Bring brilliant people out of isolation and into one room, design that room with attention, and their potential turns into momentum — people who feel seen, strangers who leave as collaborators, and ideas that finally get acted on.

![Diagram: brilliant people from different backgrounds flow into a designed room built on network effects, curation, and personalization, which produces momentum. Attention curates who comes in and creates the outcome.](/projects/555-studio/input-process-output.svg)

## Our why

> Creation isn't always something you can hold. Sometimes it's a shift — a conversation that changes direction, a question that lingers, two strangers recognizing something familiar in each other.

555 began out of curiosity about *why* someone chooses a particular path, why certain ideas stay, and what brings a person back to the same obsession again and again — what they're protecting and what they're pursuing. Every event is built around a few questions:

- Why do you do the things that you do?
- Why do you read the things that you read?
- Why do you befriend the people that you befriend?
- What are you so curious about — and why that, over anything else?

## Experiment 01 — Presentation Night

The studio's first format, and the origin of the name.

1. **Five stories** — five people each share something they genuinely care about.
2. **Five minutes** — each storyteller gets five minutes to present their passion.
3. **Five minutes of Q&A** — the audience responds in real time.

Presenters don't know who they'll be speaking in front of, and the audience doesn't know which stories will be shared. That mutual unknown is deliberate: in the moment, storyteller and audience form a relationship from scratch.

The Q&A is treated as the most important part of the night. It's where the storyteller's work is perceived live, where the audience starts to see themselves in someone else's work, and where the conversations begin that carry on long after the last slide.

## Experimenting like a product team

555 runs its events the way you'd build a product — through experiments and validation. Variables tested across nights include:

- **Venue** — different spaces and how they shape the energy in the room.
- **Audience size** — 30, 50, and up to 100 people, to find where energy leaks and where it's kept sacred.
- **Presentation formats and topics**
- **Collaborations and partnerships**
- **Lighting** — down to how each venue was lit.

The goal isn't scale for its own sake. Each experiment sharpens the studio's understanding of what creates depth, openness, and momentum.

> A studio is a place of attention. Connection shifts with space, scale, and presence. 555 evolves through attention.

## The 555 formula for community

The framework 555 uses to build high-trust, high-energy rooms:

1. **Network effects, in real life** — start with your inner circle: friends, family, people you've met along the way. Ask each of them to bring a plus one (or a plus two). From the first event to the second to the third, growth compounds.
2. **Curation** — once demand exists, choose who comes in. Ask attendees why they want to be there, both to decide if they belong in the room and to shape the event around them.
3. **Personalization** — the most important part of the night. Make every single person feel seen: ask what they care about and what they're curious about. That attention is what makes connection possible by the end of the evening.

## Inside a 555 night

Co-host Raqib Muktadir first came as an attendee. What stayed with him wasn't the presentations themselves — it was that ideas shown on stage *didn't end there*. They kept going in the room, through conversations that happened naturally afterwards.

What made 555 different early on was how motivating the environment felt. You present something you're deeply passionate about and you're met with support rather than judgment — and **being encouraged instead of judged changes how willing you are to take risks.** It pushes people to build, to share, and to act on ideas they've been holding on to.

Raqib went from attendee, to helping organize and support speakers behind the scenes, to being asked to join as co-host. After events, people regularly tell the hosts they felt seen, felt heard, and that their stories were relayed beautifully — and that it kept them going.

> 555 is not just about ideas. It is about momentum.

## Impact

In its first year and a half:

- **10+** events hosted
- **80–90** people shared personal stories on stage
- **700** collaborators attended as audience members
- Supported by partners including **Shopify** and **Planned**

The numbers matter less than the people — the room is the product.

## What's next

New experiments focused on **creative transformation** — giving artists, creators, and entrepreneurs across Canada a platform to share their work, show what they care about, and create real opportunities for connection.

The goal stays simple: *create the conditions where people feel seen, inspired, and moved to keep building.*
